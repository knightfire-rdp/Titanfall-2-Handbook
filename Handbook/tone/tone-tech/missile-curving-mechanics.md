# ℹ️ Missile-Curving Mechanics

Missile curving is by far the most complex tech for Tone, possibly even making missile barrage the most complex ability in the game. There is just so much geometry and practice at play. You'll know when you're fighting a good Tone when you get hit around difficult obstacles.

## <mark style="color:yellow;">Fundamentals of Curving</mark>

Missile curving on its own is quite a complex subject since it's not just knowing where your opponent is and where the obstacles are; there are tons of factors at play, since having the missiles reach a target is one thing, but having them _actually_ hit that target is an entirely different matter.<sup><mark style="color:orange;">**⟨1⟩**<mark style="color:orange;"></sup>&#x20;

But simply speaking, your missiles home in on your target's body center mass and can see them through walls. They struggle with sharp turns, making them [easy to dodge](../../dodging-homing-missiles/), similar to Archer. The missiles can be manipulated by looking elsewhere to take advantage of their slowish turn, letting you essentially reach steeper angles. Which lets you curve over larger obstacles, like with vertical missiles, or even manipulate missiles to hit someone else, like with missile stacking.

Missiles also have a global tracking property, meaning no matter how far away you are from the locked-on target, your missiles will still fly towards it. This is very useful to know, since holding onto the missiles for 15 seconds lets you remotely locate the enemy and know exactly where they are. Use [vertical missiles](missile-curving-mechanics.md#vertical-missile-curving) to have an even easier time finding out where the enemy is.

I'll make a rough list on how to roughly think when curving missiles. With practice, you'll only need to pay attention to a few of those.

<details>

<summary><strong>You need to be aware of the following things in order:</strong></summary>

1. Where is the enemy?
2. Visualizing the flight path of my missiles.
3. Is there an obstacle between my missiles and the enemy?
4. What angle could _reach_ the enemy?
5. Is the opponent distracted<sup><mark style="color:orange;">**⟨2⟩**<mark style="color:orange;"></sup>, or are they focused on me?
6. How should I angle my missiles to predict the opponent's movement? Or how could they dodge the missiles if there is no cover?<sup><mark style="color:orange;">**⟨3⟩**<mark style="color:orange;"></sup>
7. Do they have an ability<sup><mark style="color:orange;">**⟨4⟩**<mark style="color:orange;"></sup> to negate/block my missiles?
8. I have 15 seconds to decide when to fire; otherwise, my lock is gone.<sup><mark style="color:orange;">**⟨5⟩**<mark style="color:orange;"></sup>&#x20;

</details>

Ofc you don't need to think out every step every time, but this is just a rough example of what to consider when curving.&#x20;

#### <mark style="color:yellow;">Key Features</mark>

* <mark style="color:yellow;">Missiles home in on targets' center body mass and see them through walls.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Missiles see neither obstacles nor other Titans.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Missiles struggle at sharp turns and can be</mark> [easily dodged.](../../dodging-homing-missiles/)
* <mark style="color:yellow;">There are a ton of things that one should know about curving missiles.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Firing missiles the moment you get a lock-on is not always the best idea; you have 15 seconds to fire them thoughtfully.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Missiles have global tracking properties, so you can save them for later and fire within 15 seconds when you want to locate the target.</mark>

## <mark style="color:yellow;">Vertical Missile Curving</mark>

This is like the most important curving method by far. Due to how slowly the missiles curve, firing them backwards at an upwards angle at an enemy causes them to fly over a very high curve. And then the cool thing about missiles is that once they're vertically above an enemy, they're in a perfect position to turn sharply at the opponent, making them very difficult to dodge.<sup><mark style="color:orange;">**⟨1⟩**<mark style="color:orange;"></sup>&#x20;

This type of curving is recommended for medium to long ranges. Being too close causes the missiles to overfly due to the sharp angle and miss the enemy easily. But being too far can also be an issue since the missiles try to slowly align<sup><mark style="color:orange;">**⟨2⟩**<mark style="color:orange;"></sup> with the enemy's body center mass, meaning they want to approach the enemy from a horizontal angle. So after a long distance, they'll become normal missiles, losing their vertical approach from above and becoming much easier to dodge.

#### <mark style="color:yellow;">Key Features</mark>

* <mark style="color:yellow;">Ability to launch missiles at a higher angle and a steeper curve.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Makes the missiles approach the enemy from above, making them harder to dodge.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Firing too close makes them easier to dodge.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Firing from too far causes them to slowly go down until they're at the same height as the enemy.</mark>

The middle barrage will be fired directly in front of you, while the other 2 will branch out sideways away from the middle barrage at like 45-degree angles.

## <mark style="color:yellow;">Missile Stacking</mark>

Basically a term for having locks on a different target or multiple different targets. And using those locks to angle your missiles to hit the target you're up close to or currently fighting. This is quite useful against Ronin and Ion because they're able to easily block your missiles if they know about the lock.

This can also be used to focus damage onto a singular healthy target. Say you have multiple locks on distant enemies and you're close to an enemy. You could position yourself in such a way that the enemy Titan would be in the missiles' flight path, since missiles don't account for obstacles.

This mechanic takes a lot of practice and attention since the missiles won't go directly for the target you want to stack missiles on; they'll strictly try to reach the locked-on target but are clueless about any obstacles or Titans in the way. Use the 15-second lock-on timer to plan out your missiles flight paths and how to angle the missiles to hit the enemy itself.

## <mark style="color:yellow;">Staggered Missiles</mark>

This is more of a cool thing that you can utilize; it works noticeably better on mouse and keyboard due to the required fast flicking. But basically, since you already know about vertical curving, you must've asked yourself, “Can I combine [vertical curving](missile-curving-mechanics.md#vertical-missile-curving-medium-to-long-range) with normal curving to create a long, staggered missile barrage to force an enemy to waste time defending themselves for longer?” Yes, you can.&#x20;

This is a cool trick that I think the term itself speaks really well for. If you've played Tone a lot and experimented with missile curving, you should know that fast flicks still let you shoot from various angles. Oftentimes staggered curving is useful to force opponents into defense for longer.

Be it a player with a dash waiting to dodge your barrage or an enemy holding a shield. You can fire missiles normally first, followed by vertical missiles, making it a lot harder to dodge all of them. Staggered curving lets you extend the length of your barrage artificially. By spacing out the missiles, you cause them to feel like a longer barrage, forcing the enemy to dodge for longer or hold their defensive ability for longer.&#x20;

The staggered curving method is quite dependent on the situation since your forward-fired missiles are a lot easier to dodge, so think carefully when to do that.

#### <mark style="color:yellow;">Key Features</mark>

* <mark style="color:yellow;">Firing normal missiles followed by vertical missiles together in one barrage lets you stagger the barrage.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Staggered curving lets you prolong the duration of the barrage by spacing out the missiles.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">The enemy needs to dodge/block staggered missiles for much longer.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Staggered curving is useful for forcing the enemy to waste resources or to catch them off guard.</mark>

## <mark style="color:yellow;">Aftershock Missiles</mark>

Aftershock<sup><mark style="color:orange;">**⟨1⟩**<mark style="color:orange;"></sup> missiles are very similar to [staggered missiles](missile-curving-mechanics.md#staggered-missiles), meaning that mouse and keyboard are recommended over controller due to the fast flicking. They're in separate areas to prevent confusion due to how differently they function; despite very similar activation methods, the main difference is that their methods are inverted.&#x20;

Aftershock missiles require you to batch-fire the missiles in 2 separate bursts, basically the vertical missile burst followed by a forward-fired missile burst. Instead of spacing out the missiles one by one, you instead fire 2 clumped-up groups of missiles at separate times. So that the enemy has almost no time to react to the 2nd group, something similar to Salvo Core missile batching.&#x20;

Since vertical missiles have to cover a larger distance than [normal missiles](#user-content-fn-1)[^1]. If you fire vertical missiles first and follow them with normally fired missiles, both missile angles will hit the target at a similar time. Ofc that requires timing and practice, but in theory, you could slip in a few missiles if the enemy doesn't block fast enough. Won't do much against Ion because of her massive Vortex Shield grab range for projectiles, but could be useful against other Titans, especially those that play on controller.

#### &#x20;<mark style="color:yellow;">Key Features</mark>

* <mark style="color:yellow;">Firing vertical missiles followed by normal missiles together in one barrage lets you create a missile aftershock.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Aftershock missiles let you separate your barrage into 2 separate missile batches:</mark>
  * <mark style="color:yellow;">Batch 1: Vertical missiles from above.</mark>
  * <mark style="color:yellow;">Batch 2: Forward-fired missiles from varying angles.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">The enemy needs to be cautious of both batches, or else one will slip through and hit them.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Aftershock missiles are useful for slipping in extra damage via the enemy being unaware or being too slow to react in time.</mark>
