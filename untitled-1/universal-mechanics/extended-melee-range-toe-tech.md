# 🟢 Extended Melee Range/Toe-Tech

Due to how strong and useful this tech is, this has its page separate to [melee-tech.md](melee-tech.md "mention"). \
This should be essential to learn as a Ronin, just like [quickswap-crouch-tech.md](quickswap-crouch-tech.md "mention").

## <mark style="color:yellow;">What is Extended Melee Range/Toe-Tech?</mark> (Written by Zia)

In most FPS games, a melee attack works by checking a “hitbox” region in front of you to see if something damageable is inside it. Titanfall 2 does this too, but TF2’s melee hitbox shape is a cone with a flat base, starting with the tip of the cone near your camera and extending forward.

Because the cone’s base is flat, aiming directly at the target actually gives you the shortest melee range possible. If you aim slightly away (offset) from your target, you can hit them with the outer edge of the cone, which reaches farther than the center/base of the cone would.

How much extra range you get depends on how far you can offset your aim. Extra melee range increases range from 6.67% all the way to 20%, depending on melee type and angle.

### <mark style="color:yellow;">Max Offset</mark> (Off-Center) <mark style="color:yellow;">Angles and Ranges According to Testing</mark>

* <mark style="color:yellow;">Non-Ronin Titans</mark> (max 20° offset) {6.67% range increase}
  * <mark style="color:yellow;">No offset: 7.5m</mark>
  * <mark style="color:yellow;">With offset: 8m</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Ronin</mark> \[no Sword Core] (max 30° offset) {10% range increase}
  * <mark style="color:yellow;">No offset: 10m</mark>
  * <mark style="color:yellow;">With offset: 11m</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Ronin</mark> \[with Sword Core] (max 37° offset)<mark style="color:yellow;">\*</mark> {20% range increase}
  * <mark style="color:yellow;">No offset: 10m</mark>
  * <mark style="color:yellow;">With offset: 12m</mark>

{% hint style="info" %}
<mark style="color:yellow;">\*</mark>In Sword Core the offset is quite far at 37°. Meaning you have to offset way farther into empty space than you’d think. With that in mind, there isn’t a reliable visual guide for it, so you will have to judge the angles and spacing by eye through experience.
{% endhint %}

Though this offset can actually be done in any direction, as long as you’re aiming away from the target. A simple visual cue that can help when learning the mechanic is to aim downwards toward an enemy titan’s feet (hence the name “toe tech”).&#x20;

Although a useful visual guide, there are use cases for offsets in all directions away from your target. For example, you can aim in between multiple enemies for multi-hits. Aim away from enemies above or below you to extend your reach upwards/downwards, or [aim above enemies to deal increased knockback](melee-tech.md#angled-melee) while extending melee range at the same time.

This tech takes a lot of practice, and it only really pays off if you already have strong fundamentals (good spacing, melee timing, and movement).

You're actually able to hit a Northstar that hovers/flight-cores off some object, which would normally prevent a Ronin Sword Core from hitting them. Can't reach a Hover into Flight Core (or vice versa) stack, which is a lot higher.

{% hint style="warning" icon="square-exclamation" %}
Be warned, even though the tech can be used in all directions, aiming down is far more consistent than aiming at other sides of a Titan.
{% endhint %}
