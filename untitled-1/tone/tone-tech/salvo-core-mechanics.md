# ℹ️ Salvo Core Mechanics

Salvo Core is by far the most complex core ability in the game. Not only does it let you guide the core at the targets, but it also lets you lock onto targets behind obstacles, pull away the core to bait enemy defenses, hold it in the air, and much more. This page will cover all the mechanics in depth.

## <mark style="color:yellow;">What Is Salvo Core Capable of?</mark>

* <mark style="color:yellow;">Locking onto a single enemy/teammate Titan at once.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Breaking off lock with line of sight.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Batching missiles in the air together.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Baiting enemy defensives with duration and flicking.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Curving around obstacles.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Missiles breaking after barely missing the target.</mark>



## <mark style="color:yellow;">Locking Onto a Single Enemy/Teammate Titan at Once</mark>

The ability to free-aim the Salvo Core is obvious to everyone. But fewer people know that it locks onto people once you have a sight on them and even onto teammates. But how exactly does that work? Well, it's based on a line of sight, and it's very strict. Meaning, if you place your crosshair on an enemy/teammate Titan, you'll be locked onto that target even if you move the crosshair away, so long as you have line of sight.

The definition of line of sight is strange because it's half true. Sure, you do NEED to see the Titan yourself to lock onto them. However, once locked on, even if the Titan hides behind e-smoke or cover, as long as you look in the general direction where the Titan is, the missiles will fly towards that target. This is especially strong when the missiles are high in the air, preferably above the target, since then you can rain down the missiles to effortlessly hit a target behind a wall.

Like the title already said, “locking onto a **singular** enemy/teammate Titan.” Salvo Core can, in fact, only target one Titan at a time, but fret not, since you can easily break the lock yourself, and it also breaks automatically once the enemy Titan explodes. Use the locking-on and breaking-lock mechanic to create some interesting plays.

#### <mark style="color:yellow;">Key Features</mark>

* <mark style="color:yellow;">Essential mechanic.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Crosshair selects the target you want to lock onto.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Can target only one Titan at a time, even teammates.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Will lose lock once target dies.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">So long as the target is in your view, it will be locked on.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">If the target dares to hide behind cover or e-smoke while locked on and in general directional view, the missiles will still follow said target.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Missiles high in the air or above the target are more useful with the “behind cover” hitting ability.</mark>

## <mark style="color:yellow;">Breaking off Lock via Line of Sight</mark>

This is a very essential mechanic to be aware of. Especially if you plan to mess around with locking on and locking off targets for creative plays… or you just don't want to have your whole Salvo Core end up hitting a teammate Titan instead.

Anyways, as in the mechanic above about locking onto targets. Line of sight decides whether you hold that lock or not. Simply look sharply away to force your missiles back into free aim mode to follow your crosshair again, so you can spend the remaining time contemplating away the remainder of the 10 sec before your Salvo Core ends. Or instead you can just batch up missiles, switch targets, or stall the core for the enemy to use their defensive ability.

Breaking a lock is not necessarily a bad idea. Sometimes, especially in the heat of a fight, you lock onto a target just as you fire the core. Meaning your Salvo Core will fly in a direct line, ending up most likely being dodged by an enemy that is near cover. So break the lock and reposition your core.

#### <mark style="color:yellow;">Key Features</mark>

* <mark style="color:yellow;">Essential Mechanic.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Break line of sight to break the lock on.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Prevents misdirecting the core at teammates or into an obstacle.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Allows target switching and core stalling.</mark>

## <mark style="color:yellow;">Batching Missiles Together in the Air</mark>

The Salvo Core's ability to free aim allows you to keep all the missiles in the air since it takes time for Tone to fully fire all the missiles. Which means they end up in a long line instead of being bunched up together. This gives the enemy much more time to react to the attack and seek cover upon being hit.&#x20;

Which is precisely why a ton of good Tones gather all the missiles into the air first and use aim movements (wiggling the view sharply) to manipulate the missiles into the desired position of being all together. One of the methods to batch them up is to sharply look down at the floor and then at the sky a few times until they batch up.

The batching greatly lowers the chances for an enemy to be able to react. There is a big difference in being hit by 15+ missiles over a timespan of several seconds vs. being hit by all of them at once within a second. That burst damage is the highest of any core ability in the game.

Moreover, less knowingly, the whole force of the massive burst damage at once will also fully stop an enemy Titan's dash (similar to a Railgun shot) if it gets hit by the whole barrage at once. This is quite useful for finishing off doomed Titans (assuming they don't see the missiles beforehand) without letting them eject or to prevent them from escaping or using defensive abilities in time.

#### <mark style="color:yellow;">Key Features</mark> <a href="#key-features" id="key-features"></a>

* <mark style="color:yellow;">Move your mouse sharply in the air to group up missiles.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">They greatly reduce the enemy's time to react.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Batched missiles have the highest</mark> [<mark style="color:yellow;">DPS</mark>](#user-content-fn-1)[^1] <mark style="color:yellow;">of any core in the game.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Batched missiles can fully stop a Titan's dash if timed right.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Useful for finishing off doomed Titans.</mark>

## <mark style="color:yellow;">Baiting Enemy Defensives with Duration and Flicking</mark>

Salvo Core lasts a surprisingly long time, up to 10 sec. Then the missiles will start exploding in a sequence, starting from the first missile all the way to the last fired one. Whether you want to batch missiles or not, you should still be mindful of the duration.

Every so often it's smarter to bait an enemy to waste their defensive ability, like, for example, a Legion's Gun Shield that can tank like 60% of the damage. Gun Shield lasts a very short time, a few seconds, but it can tank a large portion of the core. So if you're in a position to stall the core (not being shot at), go for it.&#x20;

You fire the core at a Titan if you know that they have a defensive ability ready. You can quickly break the lock by looking up and batching up the missiles meanwhile or something until their defensive ability ends or they're distracted by something else. Ofc this is unnecessary for coring Tone, Monarch, and Northstar.

#### <mark style="color:yellow;">Key Features</mark> <a href="#key-features" id="key-features"></a>

* <mark style="color:yellow;">It's very useful to know “locking on,” “breaking lock,” and “missile batching” beforehand.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Salvo Core lasts up to 10 seconds.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Can flick the core away from the enemy when needed.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Most effective against Legion.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Useful when standing behind a Particle Wall or not being under fire.</mark>

## <mark style="color:yellow;">Curving Around Obstacles</mark>

With a grasp of knowing the mechanics of locking on, breaking locks, and preferably batching missiles too. You now have a solid understanding of Salvo Core movement and can use it to do good plays like curving the missiles around obstacles. Batching the missiles just makes it a lot easier to move them around obstacles or do multiple sharp turns.

Of course there are many ways to do so; unlike tracker rockets, you can't simply just move the Salvo Core away from the obstacle and magically have it find the enemy and fly towards it, NO. You require a proper lock on the enemy to find the enemy once you're past the obstacles. The easiest way is to lock onto the enemy while having your missiles in the air and have the enemy hide behind cover or e-smoke while you still look in that general direction; the core will follow them behind cover.

Another way of curving around obstacles involves using the terrain as a checkpoint. For example, on the map Relic, in the middle of the map inside the ship, there's a massive hole in the ceiling. Fly your Salvo Core through the ceiling, quite far, and then leave the building yourself. Then you can direct the high airborne core at anything outside that you can spot, though keep in mind the 10-second duration.&#x20;

There are more such cases, but the Relic one is an excellent example; it not only lets you curve around the whole ship but also somewhat hides your core from the enemies. Since it's a lot harder to notice a volley of missiles from high in the air compared to directly in front of your face. Combine that with missile batching, and you'll shock or even kill someone.

#### <mark style="color:yellow;">Key Features</mark>

* <mark style="color:yellow;">It's very useful to know “locking on,” “breaking lock,” and “missile batching” beforehand.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">The missiles will follow the enemy behind cover/e-smoke if you lock on beforehand and look in the general direction.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Another method involves using corners or edges as checkpoints. Fly the core above a building, move your Titan to the other side, and locate a target.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">The middle of Relic is a great place to practice curving core through the rooftop.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Curving through the roof also helps to discreetly batch up missiles without the enemy seeing.</mark>

## <mark style="color:yellow;">Missiles Breaking After Barely Missing the Target</mark>

This is a very irritating and common mistake. Saw plenty of Tone mains firsthand have their Salvo Core miss like 90% of the missiles on a hovering Northstar and hear an audible metal pipe drop sound in their heads. Stuff like that often happens, especially with Northstar. The issue is that once Salvo Core missiles sharply miss a target, they'll disconnect from you and become dumb, flying in a straight line until they hit a wall or something.

Northstar's hitbox becomes very janky once she hovers or uses Flight Core. Basically the hitbox ends up not correctly matching the Titan and requiring the missiles to make extremely sharp turns, and they end up not actually hitting the Northstar, assuming she's moving during said Hover or Flight Core.&#x20;

A good counter to that is to either fire Salvo Core directly below the Northstar (to have the missiles need to make fewer turns, thus hitting more consistently), hit the Northstar with batched missiles from directly above, or stall the missiles until she lands. Salvo Core lasts a bit longer than Hover, so it's fine, but it lasts shorter than Flight Core and is a lot more dangerous, so don't bother with Flight Core.

This also happens with other [Titans that make sharp turns similar to the ones used to dodge missiles.](../../dodging-homing-missiles/) The trick is to batch missiles in the air and directly drop them on their head, like how a metal pipe ends up falling to the floor. But you can also angle the missiles to approach a Titan from a different angle, mainly if a Titan is facing you and they try to run or dash to the left. Break the lock and aim the missiles to hit the target from the left side, namely intercept the Titan instead of chasing it from the side.

Though this comes with practice, opponent reading, movement reading, and own-based intuition.

#### <mark style="color:yellow;">Key Features</mark>

* <mark style="color:yellow;">A very common issue against Hover and Flight Core movement.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Janky hitbox during hover</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">Sharp-turn misses cause the missiles to disconnect and fly in a straight line until they hit something.</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">To counter Hover/Flight Core:</mark>
  * <mark style="color:yellow;">Fire directly from below.</mark>
  * <mark style="color:yellow;">Batch up missiles and hit directly from above.</mark>
  * <mark style="color:yellow;">Bait Hover with the missiles and stall until she lands (bad against Flight Core).</mark>
* <mark style="color:yellow;">To counter sharp Titan dashes:</mark>
  * <mark style="color:yellow;">Batch up missiles and hit directly from above.</mark>
  * <mark style="color:yellow;">Angle the missiles to hit from an angle that intercepts their dodge instead of chasing.</mark>
