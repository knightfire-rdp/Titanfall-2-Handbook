{% tab title="Camp 1" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Campareous</mark>

* Shields are a resource to be spent. Especially on maps like Complex or any situation where you need to push as a team, push forward first and tank damage for your team to get them into position while you have block and can't be crit.
* There is no need to peek terribly often against an enemy who is outranging you. The best example of this is Complex when the Ronin mid-peeks the stairway and eats a (blocked) Power Shot. Yes, it's blocked, but that damage still adds up rapidly.
* Melee sees regular use only against Ion and Ronin. The only time melee should be used in other matchups is if you're just shy of core or getting melee spammed. Reloading and shooting four more shots is just more DPS and also more flexible than using melee.
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Kaspy 1" %}
* If I had to give tips for Ronin, assuming the one reading this has already mastered the basic mechanics of ronins abilities and are trying to improve their general ability to fight any Titan in 1v1s, I’d say try to learn your opponents pattern.&#x20;
  * Most good players often have a pattern that they stick to, figuring out that pattern can significantly help increase your chances of making the right reads on them and obtaining the upper hand.&#x20;
  * For players that are on the lower end of the skill ceiling they often either have an obvious pattern or no pattern at all and just throw whatever they can at you due to panic.&#x20;
  * Better players however have a much harder pattern to spot and at the higher end of players they can sometimes switch playstyles if they feel like they’re being countered.
  * This mostly applies to the Ronin vs. Ronin scenario since against other Titans it’s a little less nuanced. Patterns can occur with things like timing, ability usage, ammo usage, positioning, and melee tendencies.
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Zia 1" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Lord Zia</mark>

* Use other Titans, both friend and foe, as makeshift cover in fights. If I'm in a 1v2, I'm almost always trying to position myself so that one of my enemies blocks their teammate's line of sight of me. Positioning like this also means you're already lined up to go for multi-hit arc waves. (Which you should 100% be aiming for whenever possible)
* Don't always break down walls; sometimes the enemy's wall can be very useful to leave up. Especially when you're in an outnumbered fight and you're sandwiched between enemies. Dash through the wall and use it as your cover.
* Try to get more value out of your melee when melee canceling an arc wave. Melee canceling an arc wave doesn't always mean wasting time and leaving yourself exposed. For example, if you're fighting an ion and you pull up an arc wave, but he pulls up his vortex to catch it? Dash forward and melee cancel the arc wave; the melee here gets to serve a double purpose by both canceling the arc wave and by piercing the ion's vortex.
* Utilize animation pauses/cancels. You can melee, Arc Wave, or Sword Block part of the way through your Leadwall reload, and it won't fully cancel the animation. My favorite is to reach this pauseable point and throw an aggressive melee early before the reload actually finishes.
* Just because crouch tech blocking exists doesn't mean you should always flicker it between your leadwall shots. Oftentimes mag dumping is actually the better choice.\
  Example: The enemy is preoccupied with something else and not fighting back.
* Dodge damage (with dashes) whenever possible since block only mitigates damage, and some still pierces through. Learn how to dodge dodgeable things reliably like tone rockets and Scorch shots. Even very fast abilities like Railgun or Laser Shot can be dodged with a well-timed dash. Not because you're dodging the shot itself but because you're forcing the player using it to miss.
* Pay attention to [low ground vs. high ground vertical melee](../../universal-mechanics/melee-tech.md#low-ground-vs.-high-ground-vertical-melee) usage.
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Oxy 1" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Oxy</mark>

* Ronin wins or loses on single mistakes, not chip damage; the outcome of a round depends on your ability to control your mistakes.
* Adjust your play to your teammates.
  * If your team wins the poke battle, focus on preventing enemy pushes and preserving your health. Low damage isn't a problem if you didn't throw and kept your health for key moments.
  * If your team loses the poke battle, you must create pressure. Push in, drain enemy abilities, break particle walls etc. to swing engagements. Help your team mates win their engagements if you think you'd lose the Ronin 1v1; create the Ronin 1v1 if you think you'd win it.
{% endtab %}

{% tabs %}
{% tab title="Info 3" %}

{% tab title="Camp 3" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Campareous</mark>

* If in a 1v1, pressure the ion from the start, don't disengage and let them regen energy
* Do not dump leadwall into vortex shield, if they've caught more than one shot, you want to block the reflect. If you're at point-blank range and four shots get reflected into you, you will take up to a bar of damage.
* Be careful about arcing before the Ion has spent tripwires. It's very possible that they may predict when you'll go for an arc and cost you half a bar for it.
* Many ions throw tripwires into enemy smoke to autodetonate them, be mindful when using your smoke. But at the same time, it is viable to use your smoke to bait out tripwires.
* Keep an eye on the ion's vortex shield and play a bit more safely when they have more than 45% energy, crit lasers hurt and entangled ions can do nearly two bars in as many seconds with ZPL.
* If the Ion is getting away from you at range and running, you can throw an arc with the expectation that it will be caught, but in doing so they must stand still.
* At short and medium distances, you can throw an arc into a vortex shield and then punch them as they try to catch it. No matter what they do they will be hit by one, and if they try to catch the arc but get pushed back too far they may be hit by both.
* Avoid arcing at point-blank range, Ion can outright despawn arcwaves instantly with Vortex.
* Respect the splitter rifle's ADS damage output, you can lose a bar of health rapidly against it without blocking.
* Many Ions in a scramble will throw laser shots after punches if they have enough energy for one and especially if they're an entangled Ion, it's better to be cautious.
* Don't be super quick to use all of your leadwall, use their existence as a threat and melee vortex to keep pressure without spending shots. Then spend them while the ion is low energy. The longer the fight goes where the ion has to worry about leadwall the better.
* Tripwires have strange hitboxes, if the wire doesn't appear then it is safe to walk through but sometimes if the trips are on low ground, you can also dash/crouch over them too.
* Ion can have two tripwires out at once, if at any point they do, then the first set will auto-detonate within the next few seconds.
* If Ronin punches both tripwires and a Titan at once, the Titan takes double damage. Out of Sword Core, this is not very useful unless you can land a double-double hit on two titans; however, in Sword Core, intentionally meleeing tripwires to double damage on the ion is a perfectly valid strategy. Simply beware missing the Titan but not the trips.
* If you are not meleeing or arcing, remember to hold block in core. Ion isn't terribly likely to hold vortex unless you Arc so it is easy to lose track of their energy levels. Getting crit lasered for standing still sucks. Worse if it's a [ZPL](../../ion/ion-tech/zero-point-laser-shot-zpl.md) because then you can't even get the pin on the follow-up melee on reaction.
* When fighting out of core, you can bait vortex shield by ADSing or simply holding leadwall at them as if you are about to shoot. You can also hold an arc and the melee cancel it as well.
* The two most common tripwire timings for ZPT ions if just before/after an arc or during a melee. If the trip is not popped by the arc, it can punish or dissuade a follow-up melee. If they drop trips during a melee they force a mirror of your arc/melee combo, where if you punish or try to pin the melee you eat the trip and if you don't then the punch connects.
* The three most common timings for ZPT are two or three seconds into an engagement to catch and punish an early arc, after a melee, or as a reaction punishing a melee.
* Also keep in mind that ZPL has no end lag and cannot be safely punished by anything. Simply hold block and wait to see if they melee or vortex out of it.
* It is oftentimes worth it to save phase to escape trips on reaction instead of to reload or do a safe arc. The idea behind the fight is to pressure the ion constantly and deal a third to even most of your damage through melees.
* General tip instead of a matchup-specific one, but when punishing titans for batteries shoot first and arc last as arc can doublehit embarking titans.
* When arcing vortex or flameshield, try to arc to the left or right edge of the titans as they are circular shields and do not cover the edges as well.
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Zia 3" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Lord Zia</mark>

* Positioning is everything. You want to stay at a distance where you are always one dash away from melee range.&#x20;
* From "one dash away from melee range" spacing: You can constantly threaten a melee punish if Ion uses Vortex Shield and at the same time, this range invites Ion to try and dash–melee punish your Sword Block.&#x20;
* If they try to counter your swordblock it leaves them wide open for major leadwall damage. At the same time you need to avoid being predictable with your melee punishes against their shield otherwise you’re opening yourself up to the same type of counter play.
* While meleeing vortex shield is a great way to weave extra damage in against ion; often just threatening melee without committing can lead to advantage: Sometimes just dashing forward into melee range is enough to bait Ion into dropping his Vortex Shield to counter the melee that they think is coming, letting you punish with free Leadwall damage.
* If you’re fighting a skilled Ion, you’ll often notice that when you pull up an Arc Wave, they’ll either reactively pull up Vortex Shield to block it or drop Tripwires to catch you for cheeky damage.&#x20;
* When they go for the Vortex block, you can turn this against them by using a melee cancel: dash forward and melee to cancel the held Arc Wave. The melee here serves a double purpose - it cancels the Arc Wave animation while also piercing Ion’s Vortex Shield and dealing damage.
{% endtab %}

## <mark style="color:yellow;">Ronin vs. Northstar Tips</mark>

{% tabs %}
{% tab title="Info 5" %}
This is simply a cover to make navigation easier. You can click through the different tabs to view tips done by different people.

<mark style="color:yellow;">Camp: Ronin vs. Northstar Tips from Campareous.</mark>

<mark style="color:yellow;">Oxy: Ronin vs. Northstar Tips from Oxy.</mark>
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Camp 5" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Campareous</mark>

* Ronin vs. Northstar is very different from all the other matchups in that it requires far more micro than the others. It's also annoyingly difficult when against good opponents, so the first thing to keep in mind is whether you actually need to hunt down the Northstar at all.
* If you don't absolutely need to or don't have an advantageous start to the fight, you would rather not take the fight in the first place.
* Assuming you do need to, the most important part of the fight is gap closing. Use every bit of map geometry to your advantage: dash/crouch off hills and crates, phase boost from height advantage, anything that gets you more speed.
* The Northstar at range is going to focus mostly on fully charged shots to kill dash/phase and nearly uncharged shots when they think you aren't going to block.
* If you dash too early after getting hit, you get reduced speed. If you get hit at the start, it all cancels out and you go nowhere; the middle will stop you in place, and the end will push you back significantly. Turning to a side can make it harder to be crit through block for knockback.
* If the Northstar isn't looking at you and you feel confident in when they will, mix in sprints without holding block occasionally.
* Tether traps need to be either phased, smoked, or shot to be dealt with. Low-commitment or unpunishable ways of dealing with them are best.
* As for catching the Northstar, you want to be certain you have them actually caught. Oftentimes people will start shooting leadwall as soon as they get into range, but you can't sprint while shooting, and if they shoot you back, you trade even at best most of the time.
* Closing the distance to the most effective leadwall range and then killing them or getting core in one or two mags is best instead of trying to chip them down over three or four.
* Tip for thunderstorm users too: if you get the first arc to hit, don't immediately arc again. Space them out to maximize the time they're stunned for.
* You can also bait out hovers by just dashing at the Northstar in close range; the vast majority will hover as soon as you get close.
* During Sword Core, assuming they hover on flat ground, you can still swing at and hit the Northstar's feet, but if they hover from high elevation to low, it's obviously not possible.
* Hover stacking will also stall out most of your core, so you want to avoid that as much as possible. The best way to do that I've found is to NOT activate core immediately if they normally hover and instead wait them out and activate core afterward.&#x20;
* They can't hoverstack off Flight Core, so waiting out works well.
* You only need three swings to all but guarantee the win. Core will be gained when the Northstar is at around 1 1/4 to 1 1/2 bars. Two to doom, then one more to bring them low enough that even a partial leadwall will kill.
* 0-full charge is 2 seconds; that's your best opening.
* After Flight Core ends, you can get a swing at them unpunished/unpinned.
* Remember, [toe tech](../../universal-mechanics/extended-melee-range-toe-tech.md) still applies to midair targets, just less so and at a stranger angle.
* Exceptional Northstars (basically just Ovalbasher360) will opt for 1-2 charge crit spam at close range.
* Cluster actually does some good damage, and to not disrespect it, especially if you're late into the engagement or the Northstar is looking for a cluster-punch combo.
{% endtab %}

* Use some phase tricks to close ground unexpectedly; otherwise, just get an early health advantage and play mid control.

## <mark style="color:yellow;">Ronin vs. Ronin Tips</mark>

{% tabs %}
{% tab title="Info 6" %}
This is simply a cover to make navigation easier. You can click through the different tabs to view tips done by different people.

<mark style="color:yellow;">Oxy: Ronin vs. Ronin Tips from Oxy.</mark>
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Camp 6" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Campareous</mark>

* Very player-to-player-dependent playstyle, so knowing the enemy's playstyle is most important for this matchup.
* If they have no resources, don't just dump your shots into Sword Block; pressure them with melees.
* Melee is often a provoking tool to force them to do something.
* Phase, dash, or shoot after Arc Wave; you always want to do something after an Arc Wave because you can easily get outtraded for damage.
* Rule of thumb: if they can predict your melee and then block, you'll eat 3 shots, 2 if they just react well, and 1 most of the time.
* The vast majority are going to dash back when you try to phase rip, and you can predict the backwards dash for easier rip.
* The 2nd most common way to evade a phase rip is to dash forwards; you have to account for that, like dashing back.
* You have to predict how they plan to avoid the phase rip.
* For Ronin dodging a phase rip, a Ronin doing the phase rip can't go as far as you if you both have the same amount of dashes and elevation; phase boosting backwards isn't slower than forwards.&#x20;
* During a Sword Core melee pin, you should dash sideways instead of backwards if you're not at max sword range, since you can't escape extended melee when dashing backwards. Dashing sideways will require the enemy to track you.&#x20;
* Normally you don't need to count dashes unless you see them use up 2 dashes quickly.
* Can dash sideways after they melee you and retaliate with an Arc Wave.
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Oxy 6" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Oxy</mark>

* Favour reacting over initiating; back-dashing while shooting trades positively with forward-dashing while shooting due to drop-off and opponents committing to their play, allowing you to make a play while they're anchored to a specific attack. Only 2 or 3 Ronins I've played have ever stopped a pre-planned attack if it became disadvantageous to continue, most will plough on giving you a 3-4 second window where you know exactly what they're about to do.
* Initiate only when:
  * You're losing the neutral game and need to shake it up.&#x20;
  * Or you know their abilities are down and you can punish safely.
* Maintain strict cooldown awareness; most players spam abilities off cooldown, giving you a very predictable pattern of attack for you to exploit.
* Players who don't spam off cooldown still follow rhythms; identify patterns (e.g. fake melee into arc wave, a penchant for arc waves around corners while being chased, dashing close to bait out melees etc.). It is easy to disrupt this with slight alterations in your timing, for instance a half step back before going around a corner throws off 90% of arc waves thrown blindly at chasing Ronins.
* Avoid developing patterns yourself; vary timing intentionally. I used to listen to music and time arcs to the beat, so the only way to predict it was to know what song I was listening to.
* Play to win, not to dominate: stay patient, exploit mistakes, pull batteries, and don't rush. Patience beats most opponents, even if you're mechanically worse.
{% endtab %}
{% endtabs %}



## <mark style="color:yellow;">Ronin vs. Tone Tips</mark>

{% tabs %}
{% tab title="Info 7" %}
This is simply a cover to make navigation easier. You can click through the different tabs to view tips done by different people.

<mark style="color:yellow;">Camp: Ronin vs. Tone Tips from Campareous.</mark>

<mark style="color:yellow;">Oxy: Ronin vs. Tone Tips from Oxy.</mark>
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Camp 7" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Campareous</mark>

* In a true 1v1, the Ronin wins nearly every time (playing correctly, of course) ; it's when the Ronin is on a timecrunch that things get realistically possible for the Tone to outright win.
* It boils down to basically gambling, with three minutes on the clock there's no need to gamble with rockets and so Tone dies. When in teams though, and the tone needs to die within 30 seconds though, you have to actually play around the rockets and take risks to kill her quickly.
* If the tone has locks, keep a consistent distance of about one dash. If you walk too far away the Tone will disengage, too close and the Tone can land punches without using movement.
* It is not worth it to block 40 mm shots if it costs you time in reloading or shooting. This is ofc with the idea that you're already in engagement range.
* If the Tone has locks and does punch or do anything, you can get one [blockshot](../ronin-tech/shotblock.md) off without being punished. It's riskiest with 40 mm shots but melee is a free shot every time.
* Be wary of salvo core, it does a large amount of damage very quickly and good Tones can keep it in the air as a threat for a long while. Typically, the salvo core will come as you melee the Tone so it's good to keep track of relative health.
* You don't really want to dump all four shots at once if the Tone has a wall; instead, go for some variant of 2-2 or 1-2-1 to give yourself time to react to a wall being placed.
* By that same token, Double Arc is rarely worth it to do unless you hard read that the Tone will wall after the first arc hits (common reflex among mid-tier Tones).
* Tones will often defend their walls with melees, especially in chokepoints. You can frequently bait melees by standing just outside of the wall and shooting them as they melee.
* Ronin's abilities are much stronger and have a greater impact than Tone's. So if you come to a stalemate where the Tone has a lock and is sitting behind a wall, it is perfectly fine to instead just wait out the wall and block or duck behind close cover.
* Remember how many shots the Tone has hit, missed, and spent in general. A Tone with only 4 shots can get a single lock without pulse echo. Once they spend that lock, they are then out of any way to do real damage until they reload again.
* Avoid dashing directly at Tone when they have locks, as Rockets will do considerable damage through block and stall dashes by knockback as well. Even dodging a few rockets off each salvo will help significantly.
* For teams, you have to decide what risks you can take against what Tones. This is extremely based on the individual you are fighting rather than any sort of blanket rule. The more that the Tone deviates from this, the more that you have to lean on taking risks.
  * Risks like shooting once or twice even while they have locks.
  * Anticipate the punch and go for a melee pin into block (pretty stupid, but if it works you get a free melee).
  * Anticipating that the Tone will think that you will block if they shoot rockets in that moment, and then using that moment to complete a reload stage or throw an arc even.
{% endtab %}

* If you're losing, use mobility to farm batteries and Sword Core wins this.

## <mark style="color:yellow;">Ronin vs. Legion</mark> <mark style="color:yellow;">Tips</mark>

{% tabs %}
{% tab title="Info 8" %}
This is simply a cover to make navigation easier. You can click through the different tabs to view tips done by different people.

<mark style="color:yellow;">Camp: Ronin vs. Legion Tips from Campareous.</mark>

<mark style="color:yellow;">Oxy: Ronin vs. Legion Tips from Oxy.</mark>
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Camp 8" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Campareous</mark>

* If the Legion doesn't have their gun spun up, they can do precisely nothing so dump leadwall and Arc Waves.
* If the Legion dashes and there's good cover nearby, you can arc the Legion over/from cover and earn core meter safely.
* In a 1v1, do not bother trying to pop Gun Shield unless the Legion is or is about to reload.
* If the Legion uses a dash, arc-phase smoke is an excellent safe damage dealer.
* Always come out of the phase, holding block, many Legions will try to Power Shot you as you leave phase.
* Try to get point-blank with the legion and strafe left-right, go for a single shotblock into crit to bypass Gun Shield.
* Use the phase after firing all four shots to reload, and then wait out the rest of Legion's bullets before punishing reload.
* Legions like to save their time or time Gun Shield with their reload, get pointblank or arc the shield when punishing.
* When using Temporal Anomaly, it is worth it to phase early to dodge a Power Shot.
* Sword Core out-DPSes Smart Core; however, you have less health and will likely be doomed before being able to kill the Legion. It is often the play to doom the legion and then wait out the Sword Core before going for a phase rip after Arc or baiting the dash.
* Legion cannot punch for the entire duration of Gun Shield, this includes even when it is destroyed early.
* Good legions may know how to quickdraw Power Shot, which allows them to Power Shot from an unrevved position much faster than usual. Keep this in mind when punishing out-of-position targets.
* Some may also do stall Power Shots, where it does the charge animation twice before actually firing. Never stop blocking immediately after a Power Shot to avoid getting caught by surprise.
{% endtab %}

### <mark style="color:yellow;">Oxy</mark>

* Drain Legion's ammo, punish reload.
* Don't Sword Core a Smart Core unless you have significant health.
* Farm batteries if needed.

### <mark style="color:yellow;">Campareous</mark>

* Ronin vs. Monarch in 1v1s feels more like a 50/50 match, 60/40 in favor of Monarch at worst. Map choice and tech are the biggest deciding variables here, tbh.
* The first part is getting to engagement range. Monarch's first move will always be to double siphon shields off, so either phase immediately when you reach LoS or just after the rearm completes.
* Once you're “in,” it plays like a mix of Northstar and Legion almost; good aim is paramount, and most rounds will be decided by whether you get core within one or two reloads.
* Don't shoot except for singular [Shotblocks](../ronin-tech/shotblock.md) when the monarch is shooting. Your other openings are after rocket salvo (1), melee (2), Siphon (3), rearm (3), and reload (4). The number in parentheses/brackets is the amount of shots you can safely take before the Monarch can fire XO-16 again.
* XO-16 does a lot of damage, especially to crits. It's nearly impossible to get core with only one mag, and if you don't block during shots, you will actually just die to XO-16 crits.
* If you run out of ammo, then hold block; if they siphon or reload, complete the first stage of reload. Then phase and complete reload fully.
* Once in close range, you want to save your phase for finishing your reloads safely, since giving Monarch distance is a bad idea, and she has no strong burst damage to use phase for anyway.&#x20;
* XO-16 is the biggest threat here. Rockets, on the other hand, do at most half a bar; they're basically just a Laser Shot but predictable and garbage.&#x20;
* When Monarch tries to deploy Energy Siphon, I wouldn't do anything more than 1-2 shots OR an Arc Wave, but it's a window, and the matchup requires optimizations like this for best results.
* The final tip that comes to mind is the four points where Monarch will use Rocket Salvo.
  * After using a melee
  * Punishing a melee
  * After running out of bullets
  * Immediately after rearming/siphoning
