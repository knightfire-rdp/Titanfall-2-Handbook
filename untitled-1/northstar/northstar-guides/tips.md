{% tab title="Pine 1" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Pineraven</mark>

* Basically, you want to be positioned such that you can take shots at the other team while forcing them into awkward positions if they want to aggress on you. This is true regardless of the matchup, but the exact execution differs.
* You want to either play around a corner or out of the other team’s effective range. You can get closer if you have a teammate with you.
* If you are playing far, you need to be very deliberate in how you rotate. Too close and they turn 180 degrees and kill you; too far and your teammates get 2v3ed for a bit.
* When approached, you usually back up, throw a charged shot and cluster, and break line of sight.
* You use cluster as area denial or as an animation cancel. Delaying pulls with a cluster missile is strong.
* Cluster is also useful for throwing it at a corner someone’s peeking from. You can look away, and if you get a hitmarker, you know they’re about to peek.
* The cluster missile is actually not good at killing pilots outside of a direct hit or on battery pull, but it forces movement.
* You basically want to be engaging every time you are either at long range or the enemy is distracted. The best targets are the ones actively fighting; they won’t be ready to dodge or block. Look for action commitments in particular, like executions, Vortex Shield usage, melees, etc.
* Kind of throw Tether Traps anywhere you think an enemy might go through when they’re off cooldown, since they're zero commitment; I don’t stop holding an angle to throw a Tether Trap, I just briefly flick my crosshair.
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Benyy 1" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Benyy</mark>

* Generally, the rule I like to follow is to always be doing something. It might not seem like it, but Northstar is one of the most active titans on the field.
* In terms of positioning, it’s important to remember 2 things.
  * **You Have the Best Mobility in the Game** (Apart from Phase Dash)\
    Other than Legion and Ronin up close, most things you can avoid damage from via your movement or the map.
  * **Keeping Track of Enemy Titans and Ability Usage**\
    Sometimes in team modes it's good to play back, and sometimes playing even point-blank to Titans like Legion can both be useful. However, in the example of Legion, it's critical to remember when he used Gun Shield and when he used Power Shots.
  * **Target Prioritization**\
    Generally, all damage is good as long as it's effective. Something you’ll notice a lot is people will try to stall you out a lot. You do this whether you notice it or not when you play Legion. An example would be sitting on White as Northstar, and the enemy legion is just sitting on crates, crouched so they can’t be hit but can punish you at a moment's notice. But even with that occurring, I can use my movement and go to the complete opposite side of the map and be more effective there.
* Tethers are generally in positions I feel may be helpful in the future, but I don’t give them too much thought, especially in a 4v4 game. But I do use them as often as I can off cooldown.
* The important thing to remember is that you are as effective as possible when your opportunities come, which is why aim is certainly important, but not the most important.
* Well, in worst-case scenarios, which I usually call 1vXs, I think the most essential thing is to assess the entirety of the situation. Like saying, okay, they have an Ion and a Northstar with an extra Pilot. What's my best course of action? Make sure you have a clear headspace and use every small bit of information that can give you a slight edge.
  * **Some Examples of Things Important to Note in this Scenar**io\
    Did the ion use core?\
    What health are both titans?\
    Are they together?\
    Can I mislead one of them away?\
    If they go for a battery pull, what is the best way I can punish them?\
    Can I catch the pilot in a bad position?
  * Using things like sound, teammate callouts, understanding when batteries were pulled, understanding if the enemy used core, etc., are all important things to keep track of and know.\
    At the end of the day, clutching up situations is all about composure.
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Dinos. 1" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Dinosaurd123</mark>

* Placing Tethers in a way that baits out favorable engagements or stalls people out to get away for free is definitely the main use.
  * If you're setting tethers without a clear use for when it would help you to trigger it, then you're doing it wrong.
  * You can set them to be both an exit strategy and a punishing opportunity. Place them where you can create space while also stalling them in places where you or your teammates can punish them for free.
* Tethers last a long time, and you can have 4 out at a time, so on Angel City, for example, you can have one placed just behind where you're set up. Another just before the following corner that you backline to so that you can then get free shots on the person who chases you before running, and another just after that to ensure that you get away for free.
  * Northstar vs. Ronin 1v1s are the best example of people making Ronin's life suck with good tether placement. Ronin just can't play the game when it is done right.
* You get a lot more knockback in less time with low-charge spam. Use it to get away from anyone overprioritizing damage.

### <mark style="color:yellow;">Pineraven</mark>

* You want to be positioned behind your teammates if you’re playing close to them. What you don’t want to do is play directly adjacent to your teammates. They can push out with their defenses, but if you aren’t behind them, that doesn’t help you.
* For example, if your Ronin is between you and the enemy, then they can’t properly push you. So you can play closer because they give Ronin Core if they walk up on you. Of course you give ground at that point, but you come out better overall.
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Benyy 2" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Benyy</mark>

* A scenario I can give is on Boomtown. Say I'm on the FOB side near the battery, and I’m watching mid. My Ronin is fighting mid with their Ronin and they have a legion walking up to assist. Of course I’m going to give a few shots to the Ronin when opportunities arrive, but I would be much more effective if I gave more attention to the legion out in the open.
* Generally, in 3v3s and 4v4, I prioritize helping my teammates matchups and try to avoid the Northstar 1v1 to give my teammates the biggest edge. Against some Northstars (such as Soy), you may have to be more direct and take the 1 or cut them off from certain parts of the map. But in general, my biggest rule of thumb is helping my teammates with matchups.
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Dinos. 2" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Dinosaurd123</mark>

* Conceptually it's similar to [guard dog Ronin](#user-content-fn-1)[^1] where you're taking space and holding a lane while occasionally diverting your attention just long enough to make your teammate's engagement winnable, then immediately returning to keep your target in “check.” The difference between Northstar and guard dog Ronin mentality is that you're holding space via the threat of poking power.&#x20;
  * Your focus is largely to keep enemy Northstars in check while sneaking in pokes to make your teammate's 1v1s, 1v2s, 2v3s, etc. winnable, then immediately return to pressuring the Northstar to prevent them from doing the same.
  * With proper positioning, you can use 1-2 dashes to get a line of sight and poke the ion that's holding up your legion for a bar. Then get back in position without their Northstar being able to react enough to make a counterplay, that's free value.
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Pine 3" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Pineraven</mark>

* If you can be out of Laser Shot range, Ion can’t touch you. Not necessarily worth it if it cuts you off from your team, but it means you don’t have to worry about that.
* If Ion approaches you into Laser Shot range, you need to respect that and play cover. You don’t want to go out of your way to shorten the distance.
* Ions will often vortex around corners if they’re paying attention to you. You can toss a Cluster in front of them and back up more; the flickering slows them.
* Crossmap, you’re untouchable, so Ion usually plays cover until midrange and then walks up while flickering. After you fire, they tend to stop flickering until you have a charge again.
* The critical part about flickering is that it isn’t reactable if they aren’t holding anything. Most Ions have a pattern they like to flicker in. You note the pattern and then fire when you think they’ll drop it; having something in Vortex makes reactive shots possible.
* Ion is the exception to tossing Tether Traps off cooldown, because tossing it into a Vortex Shield point-blank gives you a free crit. So you save it if you think Ion will push you.
* Be aware that mistiming your shot means you have no pressure. At that point you throw Tether Trap with Cluster and seek to disengage until you have more distance.
* If they’re hard-aggressing, you can throw a charged shot off and immediately dash around a corner. This is presuming they briefly lose vision; otherwise, don’t bother. An uncharged shot barely hurts; I only throw it out the moment before I go around a corner.
* Keep in mind how close Ion has to be to catch a cluster when helping pull a battery. If you time it closer, then Ion has to wait longer to anchor a battery.
* Against Ion and Ronin your goal is to stall as long as possible.
* Keep in mind your core; Ion can’t do anything about it. You can actually brawl if you’re close to getting it. She just dies if Laser Core isn’t up. Overcore is strong against aggressive Ions for this reason.
* So if, say, you’re 80% to core and Ion’s healthy, you can wait around the corner, use your Tether for a crit, then throw an uncharged shot or two and immediately use core. Melee works too if you’re that close.&#x20;
* If you like throwing a cluster immediately after a shot, some Ions will keep Vortex up to catch it. You can consider briefly delaying it or throwing it at the ground.
* Don’t clash cores if you know Ion’s is up; instead, play distance. If Ion pops it at a bad time and you’re behind cover, then dash out, shoot, and dash back in. There is zero counterplay from Ion.
{% endtab %}

### <mark style="color:yellow;">Benyy</mark>

* Mix up your shot timings to throw off vortex timings. Use lesser charged shots and AIM FOR CRITS as much as possible.&#x20;
  * Taking shots from different angles to take them by surprise is a great strategy as well.

{% tab title="Pine 5" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Pineraven</mark>

* Northstar duels can be thought of as having two phases. Crossmap poke and a point-blank brawl where you’re trying to dodge while landing your shots.
* The thing is, you force a brawl, and the other Northstar backs off and keeps shooting you around corners. So what you want to do is be able to shoot their teammates better than they can shoot yours. This forces them to be aggressive to throw you out of your angle. If they don’t, you just cross-shot their teammates.
* Aggression will also be forced if neither of you has an angle and their teammates are losing. They have to make something happen. This is relevant for Northstar duels, but also just in general. You want to force them to come to you.
*   Flight Core should not generally be used against Northstar, but you might have the option with the 3 things listed below:

    * You need to be point-blank.
    * They need to have absolutely zero cover.
    * They can’t be able to hover (or can be backed up against a wall for splash instead).

    If Hover is burned and she has cover, she knocks you back with a charged shot and gets behind cover. If she isn’t point-blank, she strafes with dashes and crits you.
* It is easy to land a shot against Hover or Flight Core. They have distinctive audio cues, so you want to listen for them and immediately be ready to punish.

## <mark style="color:yellow;">Northstar vs. Ronin Tips</mark>

{% tabs %}
{% tab title="Info 6" %}
This is simply a cover to make navigation easier. You can click through the different tabs to view tips done by different people.

<mark style="color:yellow;">Pine: Northstar vs. Ronin Tips from Pineraven.</mark>

<mark style="color:yellow;">Benyy: Northstar vs. Ronin Tips from Benyyfly.</mark>
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Pine 6" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Pineraven</mark>

* Ronin has a hard time pushing you, but if you make one mistake, you get punished incredibly hard.
* The two main things you should be worried about are phase boost into magdump or getting arced.
* You want to play just outside that range if possible. Too far away, and the Ronin can opt to disengage and either attack your teammate or pull. You aim to be in a position to deny that.
* If the Arc Wave is thrown midrange, then you can dodge with hover. If point-blank, then you have to do a preemptive hover, but that often gets you killed anyway if your Ronin doesn’t bail you out.
* Be cautious about activating core or hover in unreactable Arc Wave range. Getting hover-jammed is incredibly bad.
* If the Ronin gets on you and you have a charged shot, you can try to hold it to pressure the Ronin. Usually he’ll just dash to the side and throw a Shotblock or Arc Wave, so it’s not a stalemate. I like holding mine for a moment and then firing for the knockback and popping hover.
* If he’s on you, you’ve made an error and are aiming for damage control.
* The fascinating stuff happens when the Ronin is engaging someone else. If the Ronin doesn’t know you have an angle, they might drop block at a bad time and let you get a shot. When they at least suspect, they’ll at most flicker block until they get solid cover from you.
* If they’re paying attention to you, melee and Arc Wave will be covered by Phase. I don’t even bother punishing that if I know the Phase is up; it just wastes a shot. If they don’t have Phase or don’t know you’re there, it’s the single best damage opportunity you have.
* Keep in mind that as long as you hold the shot, they can’t effectively reload, melee, or Arc Wave. You can hold the shot to pressure them and then flick it into someone else while they’re backing out.
* Ground-level hover doesn’t save you from Sword Core. If you can hover off something onto lower ground, though, that can outrange it. You can also core immediately after hovering for defense.
* Flight Core stalls Sword Core pretty well. You want to wait until Ronin’s almost on you to pop it, though. Otherwise it expires slightly too soon.
* If you can’t hover away from Sword Core, throw a cluster in front of you and try to pin the Ronin into it. This is a desperation move and a hard loss to Phase/Arc Wave. Keep in mind you will also take some chip damage and might instantly die to a swing while doomed.
* If you can land a tether after Phase the Ronin kind of wastes Sword Core; you really want to aim for this if possible. But if Ronin saves e-smoke, then it doesn’t do that much, but forcing an Arc Wave or a [Leadcore](../../ronin/ronin-tech/shift-core-lead-core.md) is superb and will slow him down a lot.
{% endtab %}

### <mark style="color:yellow;">Benyy</mark>

* Maintain distance, but in team modes apply as much pressure as possible without removing the threat of damage to his teammates.

## <mark style="color:yellow;">Northstar vs. Tone Tips</mark>

{% tabs %}
{% tab title="Info 7" %}

<mark style="color:yellow;">Benyy: Northstar vs. Tone Tips from Benyyfly.</mark>

{% tab title="Pine 7" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Pineraven</mark>

* You never want to be in the open against Tone.
* Tone can’t push past the wall, so you stalemate for a bit. You lose ground, but you’re much faster, so you can just rotate away.
* You can pressure Tone if you have a teammate to shoot her after the wall is taken down. Since you lose your charge, you can’t do much by yourself after taking down the wall.
* Furthermore, you can’t dodge rockets quite as well if you’re holding a charge. So you either acknowledge the pressure or eat the damage.
* You can still sometimes dodge if they mess up the curve, though it depends on the curve. You always want to Dash.[ When dodging.](../../dodging-homing-missiles/stryder-missile-dodging.md) Cover works if it’s high enough.
* Getting hit by sonar is awful. You really want to be rotating and hitting from unexpected angles. If they have walls, you can’t do that. This kind of forces you to play more in the back, which lets the Tone walk up easier.
* You ideally beat Tone by playing around your teammates and breaking walls for them while also providing a deterrent if the Tone tries to push. You can also just play really far back, but that can have issues if you lose your angle.
* If you get cored, you want to hover and make some of it miss if pointed directly at you.
* If you have Flight Core against Tone, you want to wait for the Tone to get close to you. You would rather not eat an extra salvo closing the gap. Keep in mind that Tone can use Wall as a reaction to block a shot, so if you’re counting on that to get core, you can end up dying for it.
* If you can rotate so the wall isn’t facing you, it’s an excellent punishment opportunity. Breaking it is a concession if she’s facing you; if you can bypass it entirely, that’s ideal.
{% endtab %}

### <mark style="color:yellow;">Benyy</mark>

* I prefer using the Overcore kit whenever I’m taking a consistent 1v1 with the Tone. Sometimes in 3v3, when there is no enemy Northstar, I'll go to Overcore as well.
* In team modes, break the Particle Wall as much as possible with a full charge shot followed by Cluster Missile.
* Try to punish as much as possible when she doesn't have Particle Wall and is exposed.

## <mark style="color:yellow;">Northstar vs. Legion Tips</mark>

{% tabs %}
{% tab title="Info 8" %}

<mark style="color:yellow;">Pine: Northstar vs. Legion Tips from Pineraven.</mark>

<mark style="color:yellow;">Benyy: Northstar vs. Legion Tips from</mark> <mark style="color:yellow;">Benyyfly.</mark>
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Pine 8" %}
### <mark style="color:yellow;">Pineraven</mark>

* You really want to be rotating against Legion. He’s really slow, and if he’s keeping the gun revved while rotating, you’re winning already. If he doesn’t, you want to constantly be switching angles and harassing him.
* This is another matchup where Northstar gets punished really hard for making a mistake.
* You always want to be out of close-range Power Shot distance, either via cover or distance. Long-range Power Shot isn’t particularly difficult to dodge but will often land against a peek prediction or hover.
* Hover also gets absolutely melted by regular long-range fire. I try to only do it to peek at an angle the Legion isn’t ready for. Of course, Hover also gets melted at close range. But why on Earth would you hover that close to a Legion?
* If you can shoot immediately after hovering and move behind a building/wall for the rest of the duration, Legion usually cannot get a good punish for it.
* You can shoot toes or shoulders when Gun Shield is up, provided you have the appropriate angle. This works significantly better when the Legion is distracted or backing up. If he’s focused on you, you can end up eating a Power Shot for it. It’s also really difficult to assess the angle before the shield is up.
* I like playing very passively in Legion. He has to use plenty of resources defensively, and you have much stronger poke. If the Legion aggresses you, simply dash at him from halfway across the map. He can’t dodge to save his life.
* If Legion holds an angle against you, I generally favor rotating out entirely. You would rather not take a chance with that; the rotation is typically completely free because of how much faster you are.
* The main thing Legion can do on tighter maps is push your teammates, which will often make the Northstar push up behind him to get a punish. Legion can then spin around and take 2 bars completely for free.
* You can use Exoplanet silos and Glitch columns to do the very funny thing of simply walking around them and going ring-round-the-rosey; it works surprisingly well on weaker Legions.
* Flight Core is quite sketchy against Legion; you obviously die if you try to core a revved Legion with full resources, but even one without such things can punish you.
* You core a reloading Legion, take 2 bars, he spins around, pops gun shield and power shot, and you die if you’ve taken damage earlier on. If you have the health, it can sometimes be worth it to close the kill. But you accept that you’re taking a ton of damage for it.
{% endtab %}

* Keep track of Power Shot and Gun Shield cooldowns to know when it's safer to play more aggressively.&#x20;
* When Legion is out in the open, punish as much as possible.

* Monarch has very little burst damage, so you really want to favor corner peeks against her.

* You always want to trade into Siphon when possible. You still get around half a bar, and the moment you get core, the Monarch dies.
* The shields disabling crits is annoying, though. Keep that in mind if you’re counting on a crit to get core.
* I again want to stress that she can do absolutely nothing to you as long as you play corners.
